# Waypoint description sources

Where the facts in each trail's `data/trails/<dir>/descriptions.json` came from (issue #30). Every statement in those files should trace to a source listed here; where sources disagreed, the note says which one was followed. Retrieved 2026-10-19. Add a section when another trail gets a file, and update the trail's section when its text changes.

Trails authored before this note (Cape to Cape, AAWT, Bibbulmun, Hume & Hovell, Larapinta) have no source list.

## Three Capes Track (`three_capes`)

- Parks & Wildlife Service Tasmania, [Three Capes Track walk notes](https://parks.tas.gov.au/explore-our-parks/tasman-national-park/three-capes-track/walk-notes): boat from Port Arthur (up to 75 minutes), the wade at Denmans Cove (knee-deep at times September–December), day-by-day route (Surveyors view to Cape Raoul, Arthurs Peak over Crescent Bay, Crescent Mountain, Ellarwey Valley, Munro Bight and Cape Hauy, The Blade and Tasman Island, Cape Pillar out-and-back from Munro, Retakunna and Mount Fortescue, Cape Hauy's dolerite columns and the Totem Pole), the 30-minute bus from Fortescue Bay.
- PWS Tasmania, [Three Capes Track FAQ](https://parks.tas.gov.au/explore-our-parks/tasman-national-park/three-capes-track/frequently-asked-questions): cabin facilities (48 beds, gas cooktops, heated hub, toilets; showers at Munro only), "Rainwater is available from tanks at each cabin … treat the water", mobile reception at each cabin.
- PWS Tasmania, [Fortescue Bay camping](https://parks.tas.gov.au/explore-our-parks/tasman-national-park/fortescue-bay-camping): Banksia and Mill Creek campgrounds, tank water ("boiling for 3 minutes or sterilising is recommended"), token showers at Banksia, Mill Creek's larger sites and generators until 9 pm, site office supplies, Nubeena 45 minutes away, booking season. Note: the page's feature list also says "Drinking water"; the description follows the body text.
- PWS Tasmania, [Cape Pillar](https://parks.tas.gov.au/explore-our-parks/tasman-national-park/tasman-cape-pillar): Bare Knoll (about 1 km from the Old Cape Pillar Track crossroads, 12 tent platforms, toilet, natural water not to be relied on, register at Fortescue Bay), cabins for Three Capes walkers only, Mount Fortescue's wet forest.
- PWS Tasmania, [Cape Hauy](https://parks.tas.gov.au/explore-our-parks/tasman-national-park/cape-hauy): dolerite columns plunging into the sea.
- Bibbulmun Track Foundation, [The Three Capes Track, Tasmania](https://www.bibbulmuntrack.org.au/the-three-capes-track-tasmania/): "Pillars of the South" as an interpretive stop about the dolerite columns.
- Traversing Tasmania, [Three Capes Track: Day 3](https://traversingtasmania.com/tag/the-blade/) (walker's blog): Seal Spa seats and its view of Tasman Island's tramway, lighthouse and three houses; The Blade's formed steps and views.
- Tasmania.com, [Cape Hauy hike](https://tasmania.com/activity/cape-hauy-hike-active-adventure/) (tour operator): the Candlestick and Totem Pole.
- OSM / Copernicus via the committed GPX: summit heights (Arthurs Peak ~300 m, Mount Fortescue ~490 m), the names Monument Lookout, Pillars of the south and Seal spa, and the Munro "Drinking water" node.

Weakly sourced: **Monument Lookout** (no source names it; the text describes Cape Hauy from its position on that spur), **Pillars of the south** and **Seal spa** (third-party pages only).

## Thorsborne Trail (`thorsborne`)

parks.qld.gov.au answered 403 to direct fetches from the authoring session, so these facts come from the Queensland Parks pages as quoted in search results, not from a read of the full pages. Worth re-checking against the live pages.

- Queensland Parks and Wildlife Service, [Thorsborne Trail — walking](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/hinchinbrook-thorsborne/walking): leg distances, water per leg (Nina Bay creeks 100–200 m upstream; creek upstream of the Little Ramsay Bay lagoon; Banksia Creek 100 m upstream; Zoe Creek about 600 m upstream; Fan Palm and Cypress Pine creeks most reliable on that section; water at Mulligan Falls; Moth Creek seasonal; none at George Point), the granite saddle at 260 m from South Zoe Creek.
- QPWS, [Thorsborne Trail camping](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/hinchinbrook-thorsborne/camping/thorsborne-trail): facilities per camping area (composting toilets, platforms, pack poles/rack), Zoe Bay's separate boat-based and day-use areas, Mulligan Falls as the sixth area from the north.
- QPWS, [Banksia Bay camping area](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/hinchinbrook/camping/banksia-bay): no facilities, no toilets, no campfires.
- QPWS, [Hinchinbrook Island National Park](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/hinchinbrook) and [Hinchinbrook Island Discovery Guide (PDF)](https://parks.des.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/165421/hinchinbrook-discovery-guide.pdf): Zoe Falls about 15 minutes from the South Zoe Bay day-use area; Mulligan Falls restricted access area, deaths and serious injuries, base for Thorsborne walkers only.
- QPWS, [Visiting safely](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/hinchinbrook-thorsborne/visiting-safely): boat access to Ramsay Bay and George Point, George Point reached from Dungeness (Lucinda). Its park alert (George Point toilet removed for erosion, to 30 June 2027) is why the George Point entry says "no facilities".

## Six Foot Track (`six_foot_track`)

- NSW National Parks, [Six Foot track](https://www.nationalparks.nsw.gov.au/things-to-do/walking-tracks/six-foot-track) and [visitor info](https://www.nationalparks.nsw.gov.au/things-to-do/walking-tracks/six-foot-track/visitor-info): 1884 horse track, start near the Explorers Tree on Pulpit Hill Road with parking there, toilets at Old Ford Reserve, Cox's River and Black Range and none at Alum Creek.
- NSW National Parks, [local alerts](https://www.nationalparks.nsw.gov.au/things-to-do/walking-tracks/six-foot-track/local-alerts): Jenolan Karst Conservation Reserve closed until 31 December 2026 (not in the descriptions — it is temporary).
- Visit NSW, [Six Foot Track](https://www.visitnsw.com/destinations/blue-mountains/katoomba-area/six-foot-track): start about 2 km outside Katoomba, the name from the track's width, Jenolan's cave system at least 340 million years old.
- sixfoottrack.com, [Track notes](https://www.sixfoottrack.com/track-notes/) (the site NSW National Parks links as the track's website): Explorers Tree history, the information shelter, Norths Lookout, Nellies Glen steps, Old Ford Reserve, Coxs River campsite and its water, Bowtells Swing Bridge (1992), Alum Creek (least developed, no facilities, Little River), Black Range campsite and its tank, the descent to Jenolan, Caves House (1898) and its services.
- sixfoottrack.com, [Alum Creek to Black Range](https://www.sixfoottrack.com/article/8189/): water at Alum and Little creeks except in prolonged dry periods, none until the Black Range tank, covered picnic table at Black Range. Note: this page says Alum Creek has a pit toilet; NSW National Parks and the track notes say it has none, and the description follows NSW National Parks.
- Wildwalks, [Farnell Road to Nellies Glen Lookout](https://wildwalks.com.au/bushwalking-and-hiking-in-nsw/blue-mountains-katoomba/farnell-road-to-nellies-glen-lookout-return.html) and bushwalkingnsw.com: Therabulat Lookout's Megalong Valley view and alternative spelling.
- Katoomba's services are kept from the waypoint's earlier hand-reviewed text.

No entry: **Mount George** (no source beyond a signpost).
